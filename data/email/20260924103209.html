Groq — in its own words (qwen/qwen3.8-27b)

On listing (15). The IPO is currently undersubscribed at 0.92x as of the latest data point, indicating weak initial demand despite the offer being open. The valuation is priced at a premium to the industry benchmark (48.05x vs 47.12x) and significantly above most peers, which likely deters retail and institutional interest in the absence of a strong growth narrative.

On the long term (45). The business exhibits low revenue growth (2.65% CAGR) and thin profit margins (3.64%), suggesting limited pricing power or operational efficiency in a competitive market. While cash conversion is strong (4.25x PAT), the high valuation relative to peers and stagnant top-line growth makes the long-term risk-reward profile unattractive for new capital.

On governance (60). The prospectus notes that promoter shares are pledged to 360 ONE Prime Limited, which is a standard but notable governance flag indicating potential leverage or liquidity needs within the promoter group. There are no explicit red flags regarding conflicts of interest with suppliers, but the pledge reduces the quality of the promoter stake.

  • Valuation Premium — The IPO is priced at 48.05x P/E, which is higher than the benchmark (47.12x) and significantly higher than key peers like Kajaria (30.48x) and Somany (19.8x), leaving little margin for error. [valuation, page N/A] (could not be verified)

  • Promoter Pledging — Promoter shares are pledged as security for a loan, which can signal financial stress or limit the promoter's ability to support the company in downturns. [capital_structure, page 109]

NVIDIA — in its own words (moonshotai/kimi-k3)

On listing (30). Demand is genuinely weak: with hours to close the book is only ~0.92x subscribed (subscription_history through 2026-09-24), and there is no anchor/GMP data to suggest smart-money support. Priced at 48x latest basic EPS against a benchmark of 47x and named peers at 30.5x, 19.8x and 8.5x, the pricing leaves nothing for the buyer, so a weak or negative debut is the base case unless last-day QIB/retail flow surges.

On the long term (40). This is a low-margin tile/sanitaryware business: PAT margin of 2-3.6% on ~2.65% two-year revenue CAGR [business, page 212], which is barely above nominal stagnation for a manufacturing company. Positives exist — operating cash flow has exceeded reported profit (conversion 4.25x), debt/equity has fallen to 0.44 and current ratio is 1.67 — but none of that justifies 48x earnings when ROE/ROCE are not even printed in the document.

On governance (45). Promoter shares are pledged to 360 ONE Prime as security for a loan to a promoter-group entity (Varmora International) [capital_structure, page 109], and further pledging is contemplated [capital_structure, page 121] — leverage at the promoter level secured on listco stock is a classic red flag. Related-party disclosures are referenced but the extracts given do not show their quantum [promoters, page 298], so I cannot clear or condemn beyond the pledge.

Key risks it identified

  • Undersubscribed book — At 0.92x late on closing day, allocation risk is low but aftermarket support is unproven; weak books often list flat or below band. [business, page 212]
  • Promoter share pledge — Pledged promoter stock backing a group-entity loan [capital_structure, page 109] creates forced-sale and margin-call risk that can hit the listed price regardless of operations. [capital_structure, page 109]
  • Wafer-thin margins in a commoditised industry — At 2-3.6% PAT margin, small input-cost or demand shocks erase profit; the document itself warns insurance and competition may not protect it [business, page 242]. [business, page 242]
  • Supplier concentration despite wide network — The prospectus admits dependence on top-10 suppliers for certain raw materials [business, page 241], so the '500+ suppliers' claim overstates diversification for critical inputs. [business, page 241]
  • Stagnant growth at a premium valuation — 2.65% revenue CAGR [business, page 212] at a 48x P/E priced above Kajaria (30x) and Somany (20x) means the multiple, not the business, is doing the work. [business, page 212]
  • Subsidiary carve-out late in the process — Simola Tiles LLP ceased to be a subsidiary effective September 5, 2026, weeks before listing, yet capacity figures include it [business, page 232] — investors may be underwriting assets no longer fully owned. [business, page 232]

What would change its mind: Evidence that the book closes meaningfully over 1x with institutional (not just retail/HNI-financed) demand, plus disclosure of ROE/ROCE and removal of the promoter pledge, would soften the negative listing view and make the long-term case worth revisiting. Conversely, ROCE above ~15% sustained through a downcycle would be needed to argue 48x is anything other than seller-favourable pricing.
