The price band is narrow, but the late surge suggests the issue is not oversubscribed in a meaningful way, which typically leads to a flat or negative listing pop.

On the long term (40). While revenue has grown significantly (from ₹536.55m to ₹2,028.94m), the operating cash flow is negative in the latest two years (-₹147.32m and -₹100.52m), indicating that the reported EBITDA growth is not translating into cash. This suggests heavy working capital absorption, likely in inventory or receivables, which is a red flag for sustainability and quality of earnings.

Retail-specific and anchor data were not available, so I cannot tell how much of that 1.37x is QIB-driven versus genuine broad demand. No pop signal here, but the thin demand is not a guaranteed flop either.

On the long term (35). Revenue of ₹536.55 million growing to ₹2,028.94 million in two years [business, page 216] with EBITDA following looks impressive, but operating cash flow is deeply negative in the two latest years (-147.32 and -100.52 vs positive EBITDA), meaning the reported profits are not arriving as cash. Classically that pattern is inventory and receivables ballooning ahead of sales — in a gems/jewellery business, profit booked on stock that has not converted. Until the cash conversion turns, the growth is accounting, not economics.

On governance (55). No disqualifying evidence: no SEBI/exchange disciplinary actions against Promoters in five years [litigation, page 453] and stated absence of conflict of interest with lessors, suppliers and related parties [promoters, page 279]. But there is a flagged inconsistency: the document prints debt-to-equity of 0.29 where an independent computation gives ~0.021 — a minor but real quality-of-disclosure mark against, and the extracts give no positive evidence (audit debate, board quality) to score higher.

Key risks it identified

  • Profits not converting to cash — Operating cash flow was negative in the latest two years despite EBITDA rising to ₹309.74 million, so earnings quality is unproven and the IPO may in substance be funding working capital, not growth capex. [business, page 216]
  • Hyper-growth ahead of demand proof — Revenue roughly quadrupled in two years, which in jewellery usually means aggressive stocking or channel credit, and reversals in either hit margins and cash simultaneously. [business, page 216]
  • Dependence on skilled labour — The company itself flags that inability to retain karigars could materially and adversely affect results — a structural fragility in Surat's tight jewellery labour market. [business, page 235]
  • Disclosure inconsistency on leverage — The prospectus prints debt-to-equity of 0.29 while the balance sheet arithmetic implies ~0.021; a seller-prepared document with unreconciled numbers warrants discounting its other claims. [promoters, page 279]
  • Fresh issue vs OFS split unknown — Whether ₹1,084 million is going into the business or into promoters' pockets could not be read from the offer details, and that single fact changes what the IPO is. [litigation, page 473]
