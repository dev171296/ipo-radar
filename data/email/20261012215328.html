On listing (50). There is no subscription data, anchor book, grey-market signal, or basis-for-price chapter in what I was given, so there is genuinely nothing to form a listing-day view on. Indian mainboard IPOs are often priced for the seller, and with no valuation anchor I default to no view.

On the long term (62). The underlying business looks real: revenue grew from ₹3,729 million to ₹4,892 million across FY2024–FY2026, the order book roughly doubled to ₹1,586 million, free cash flow is positive and rising, and the top-10 customers are only ~24–28% of revenue, which limits concentration risk [business, page 212][business, page 216]. The score is capped because I have no ROE, ROCE, margins, or balance-sheet debt figures, so I cannot tell whether this is a high-quality business or a capital-hungry low-return one at a seller-friendly price.

On governance (60). No material SEBI or regulatory inspection findings are disclosed [litigation, page 366], and related-party dealings appear confined to disclosed director remuneration and shareholding [promoters, page 278]. This is only a modest positive because the prospectus is the seller's own document and I have no detail on the scale of related-party transactions.

Key risks it identified

  • Valuation unknowable — No basis-for-price chapter or ROE/ROCE was extractable, so the ₹258–271 band cannot be tested against any return metric; overpaying is the single biggest IPO risk. [business, page 212]
  • Leased premises including corporate office — Operations including the corporate office run from third-party premises, so loss of those leases could disrupt the business that justifies the valuation. [business, page 245]
  • Order book conversion — The growth story rests partly on an order book that nearly doubled to ₹1,586 million; if margins or execution lag, the FY2026 revenue plateau (₹4,890m vs ₹4,328m) looks weaker than the headline. [business, page 212]
