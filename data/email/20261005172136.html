NVIDIA — in its own words (moonshotai/kimi-k3)

On listing (72). Subscription ended around 1.37x after being nearly flat for most of the window and only crossing 1x on the final day (subscription history at 2026-10-05), which means the book got filled but there is no frenzy — typical of a small ₹1,084m mainboard issue that will likely list modestly positive or flat rather than pop hard. Final-day surges are often driven by applications chasing allotment in a thin book; with roughly 27% of demand excess, downside is cushioned but upside at listing looks limited.

On the long term (38). Reported revenue roughly quadrupled in two years to ₹2,028.94m [business, page 216] and EBITDA rose sharply, yet operating cash flow was negative in two of three years and deeply negative (−₹147.32m) in the most recent, so the profit is not arriving as cash. Rapid growth in a working-capital-heavy gems business with profits stuck in current assets is exactly the profile where reported earnings can be flattering; the burden of proof is on cash conversion, which is currently −0.48 of EBITDA.

On governance (55). The documents disclose no SEBI or exchange disciplinary actions against promoters in the last five years [litigation, page 453] and make standard no-conflict-of-interest declarations over suppliers and lessors [promoters, page 279], but there is one unexplained discrepancy: the prospectus prints debt-to-equity of 0.29 where recalculation gives 0.021. That single mismatch is a small but real data-integrity flag in a document written by the seller.

Key risks it identified

  • Profits not converting to cash — Operating cash flow was −₹147.32m in the latest year against EBITDA of ₹309.74m, so the reported growth is being absorbed by working capital and could reverse or require external funding. [business, page 216]
  • Working-capital dependence on receivables/inventory — High current ratio (4.41) alongside negative operating cash flow indicates money is piling into current assets, which in jewellery also carries gold-price and counterparty risk. [business, page 229]
  • Financial-disclosure inconsistency — The printed debt-to-equity (0.29) disagrees with the recalculated figure from the same statements (0.021), which undermines trust in the seller's own numbers. [litigation, page 453]
  • Subscription was back-loaded and thin — Demand was under 0.11x two days before close and only finished ~1.37x, suggesting no institutional conviction and a listing dependent on retail momentum. [business, page 210]

Answers to the questions our own scoring raised

  • where did the money go — receivables, inventory, or both?
    The figures prove the money went into net working capital: EBITDA of ₹309.74m coincided with operating cash flow of −₹147.32m and a current ratio of 4.41, meaning current assets ballooned as revenue quadrupled. But the extracts do not break out the receivables and inventory lines, so I cannot confirm whether it is customer credit or gold/stone stock — in a gems business either is plausible, and the distinction matters because inventory carries commodity-price risk while receivables carry customer-default risk. not answerable from what I was given
