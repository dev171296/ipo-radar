NVIDIA — in its own words (moonshotai/kimi-k3)

On listing (45). Subscription reached only ~1.5x by late morning of day one and ~3x by early afternoon (no night/end-of-day data), which is lukewarm for a ₹1,055m mainboard issue; no anchor, GMP, or retail data was given to support a pop. Nothing here evidences strong demand, and Indian IPO subscription typically builds on the last day, so day-one numbers alone sit just below neutral.

On the long term (40). The business is cash-generative (FCF positive all three years, cash conversion 0.76) and deleveraging (debt/equity down from 0.93 to 0.41) with improving margins, but revenue CAGR is only ~6% and the latest year grew just 4.5% — slow for an IPO asking new money. Receivable days stretched from 94 to 118, meaning growth is being financed through customer credit, which contradicts the quality of earnings.

On governance (45). Personal guarantees of directors secured a USD loan [promoters, page 417], and a promoter group exemption letter suggests regulatory carve-outs [promoters, page 389]; the prospectus also reports no loans to promoters and no struck-off company dealings [promoters, page 446]. A deleted ₹37.75m income-tax penalty [litigation, page 491] means a dispute existed even if resolved in the company's favour. Thin evidence either way.

Key risks it identified

  • Customer/distributor dependence with opaque concentration — The prospectus refuses to name its top-ten customers or their contributions [business, page 334], while flagging that cessation of orders could materially hurt results [business, page 333]. [business, page 334]
  • Receivable stretch — Collection period rose from 94 to 118 days over three years, so reported EBITDA increasingly sits in receivables rather than cash even though conversion is still 0.76. [business, page 305]
  • Past tax penalty — A ₹37.75m income-tax penalty existed and was deleted only on ITAT appeal in March 2022, indicating aggressive tax positions in the recent past. [litigation, page 491]
  • Low growth at IPO price — Revenue grew ~4.5% in the latest year; at such growth the issue price only works if the seller got a rich multiple, which the data given cannot confirm because no EPS/valuation rows were available. [business, page 305]
